Why Polished Drafts Still Get Rewritten

Origin

How it started

The brief was to stop deconstructing products and start building one. Pick a product I use often, find one real friction, and design an AI-powered improvement I could actually prototype and demonstrate.

I kept landing on the same moment in my own week: an AI assistant hands me a clean draft, and I rewrite it anyway. The sentences are correct, but they are not mine, and they read the same whether I am writing to a customer or to my manager. That gap, between a polished draft and a sendable one, became the project.

What I built. Kural is an AI email assistant that drafts in your voice and reads the situation before it writes. It classifies who you are writing to and why, holds a persistent model of how you sound, and produces a draft you can send without a rewrite. I built it solo, shipped it, and it is live at kural-iota.vercel.app.

The assignment

Design and prototype an AI-powered feature

The assignment asked me to act as the PM at a company whose product I use often, identify one meaningful opportunity to improve it with AI, and design and prototype how that improvement would actually work. I focused on a single P0 problem and built a demonstrable prototype. The brief also required me to validate the thinking with PMs from that industry rather than inside the assignment.

Proving Voice Is the Blocker, Not Grammar

Survey and interviews

Where the time and friction actually go

I ran a 36-person survey through friends, WhatsApp and Reddit, plus four interviews. The question was simple: when you write an important email, where does the time and the friction actually go?

71%of AI tool users cannot copy-paste the draft: it sounds like a robot and lacks their voice
42%say getting the tone right is the single hardest part of writing an important email
75%spend five minutes or more on a single important email, and many far longer

What the numbers pointed to

Voice is the blocker, not grammar. The complaint was never that drafts were wrong. It was that they did not sound like the person sending them.

Tone is situational. The same person writes differently to a customer, a manager and a professor. Generic tools flatten all three into one register.

The work hides in wording, not typing. A third of respondents said their time goes into wording and tone, and a quarter into second-guessing what they already wrote.

Validation

Cross-checked against published research

A 36-person survey finds a signal; it does not prove one. So I held each finding against larger, published studies before writing it into the problem statement. The survey and the literature agreed.

McKinsey. Puts the professional workweek spent on email at around 28%. The time cost is real and large.

Bynder. Found 62% of readers distrust copy that reads as bot-written, and 82% accept it once it feels human. The voice problem is a trust problem.

My own survey. 42% named tone as the hardest part and 75% spend five minutes or more per important email. The friction is specific and measurable.

Meet Kural

The product

A composer, not an autopilot

You give Kural the gist in your own words. Before it writes a single line, it classifies the situation: how urgent it is, the power dynamic, and the emotional register. Then it drafts in your voice, shows you what it detected, and lets you adjust rather than regenerate from scratch.

The draft below is one pass. No "I hope this email finds you well." No padding. A message the sender could send as is.

How Kural Reads the Situation Before It Writes

The engine

Classify first, compose second

I designed the engine to classify before it composes. Urgency, power dynamic and emotional category are settled in the schema before a single line is drafted, so tone is a decision the system makes on purpose rather than something the model improvises.

  1. 1
    Classify first. A situation object carrying urgency, power dynamic and emotional category is required by the schema before a draft can exist at all.
  2. 2
    Adapt to the reader. Writing upward, to a customer, or to a peer each pull a different register, chosen by situational rules with a defined precedence.
  3. 3
    Draft in your voice. The draft is composed in that register, with an anti-cliche guard stripping stock AI openers at both the prompt and the server.
  4. 4
    Learn from your edits. Style memory updates from the changes you make, so later drafts drift towards how you actually write.

The result. A draft that reads the room, and reads like you.

Under the hood

Deliberately simple, structured underneath

Classifies before it writes. A situation object is required in the schema before a draft exists, so tone is a decision, not an afterthought.

Adapts to who is reading. Writing upward, to a customer, or to a peer each pull a different register through rules with set precedence.

Never opens with a cliche. A prompt-plus-server guard removes "Hope you are doing well" and its relatives. Ever.

Metrics
