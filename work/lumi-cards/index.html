A Lookup That Costs Minutes and Carries Exposure

How it started

Five years behind a branch desk

A client asks what a card actually covers. Answering means leaving the conversation to search a dense PDF, then saying a number out loud from memory with nothing to back it up.

The product. Lumi Cards is an assistant for personal bankers and tellers, built into the portal they already use. It answers from approved documents only, with a source link and an effective date on every figure.

Who asks, and what it costs

Branch labour spent on lookups

Personal bankers handle multi clause questions where a wrong answer carries compliance exposure. Tellers handle higher volume, single fact lookups.

Why Retrieval Was the Only Viable Approach

Alternatives rejected

Three options, three failures

AlternativeWhy it fails here
Better instructions to the modelIt cannot reach the policy documents, so it invents sources.
Fine tuningBakes in rates that change monthly and leaves no document traceability, which fails an audit.
Keyword searchReturns whole documents, not answers, and breaks on vocabulary gaps.

The vocabulary gap

Staff and documents use different words

Staff say

"Foreign conversion fee"

Document says

"FX transaction mark-up"

A keyword search returns nothing useful at the moment it is needed.

The decision. Retrieval is the only approach that meets both binding constraints: the corpus changes continuously, and every answer must trace to an approved source document.

How It Works: Two Lanes, One Index

The decision that does most of the work

Tags narrow the documents before the model sees any

Every chunk carries the card name, document type, province, effective date, expiry date and source link. The system filters on those tags at query time instead of asking the model to behave. An expired Quebec rate is not discouraged, it is out of reach.

1 · Before the search

Client data and out of scope questions are blocked.

2 · During the search

Tags filter first, two search methods run together, the best five pass on.

3 · After the answer

Every cited source is checked against the chunks supplied.

Who grades. Credit card subject matter experts, being compliance officers and senior bankers, grade a sample against a rubric for accuracy, completeness and citation correctness. After 100 to 200 reviewed traces become an evaluation SOP, an LLM judge takes over continuous scoring, with sampled expert review kept.

The set is structural at this stage. Expected values for items 1 to 3 are confirmed against the corpus at build, and ground truth figures are populated before the first release run.

Guardrails

Four refusal states, each decided before the model writes

Refusal is the designated outcome in every state. Partial answers with qualifying caveats are not permitted.

Client data

Blocked before anything else

The data screen stops account numbers and SINs before embedding, retrieval, generation or logging.

Out of scope

Redirect, not a dead end

The intent classifier blocks non card products with zero vector searches and links the right library.

Ambiguous

One question, then retrieval

When card or province is missing and the answer varies by either, Lumi asks once before searching.

Low confidence

No answer, human handoff

Below the reranker threshold, generation is suppressed and escalation to Internal Branch Support is offered.

Two checks run after generation. Citation validation and a numeric grounding check. An answer that fails either is blocked before it renders and routed to escalation. Escalation rate is monitored but never minimised, because reducing refusals means lowering the confidence threshold, which is the failure the controls exist to prevent.

Failure modes

Six ways it breaks, and the fix for each

The worst case is a correct citation with a wrong number. The source checks out and the link works, so staff read a wrong figure with full confidence. Each mode below is paired with how it is detected and what fixes it, because an undetectable failure is not a managed one.

1

Correct citation, wrong number

Critical
Detected by
A numeric grounding check. Every figure, rate, percentage and duration must appear verbatim in the retrieved context after normalisation.
Fixed by
Any response stating a value absent from context is blocked and escalated, not rendered.
2

Split clause misleads

Critical
Detected by
A coverage and exclusion pair audit, a golden probe, and a sibling completeness check before generation.
Fixed by
Exclusions link to their parent clause through a shared Clause_Group field. An incomplete group blocks the response.
3

Phantom citation

Critical
Detected by
A citation validator comparing every cited chunk ID against the chunks actually supplied.
Fixed by
The response is blocked before rendering, so a fabricated source cannot be displayed.
4

Expired document quoted

High
Detected by
Daily reconciliation of SharePoint last modified against last indexed timestamps, alerting on drift beyond 24 hours.
Fixed by
An active date restrict makes expired documents unreachable. A newer edition auto expires the prior one, and the cache flushes on re-ingest.
5

Over-trust and low click-through

High
Detected by
Citation click-through rate read against the thumbs down rate. Low click-through at high usage is the dangerous reading.
Fixed by
Citations render inline, with the effective date on the face of every response.
6

Low confidence answer shown

Managed
Detected by
A reranker confidence threshold check on every in scope query.
Fixed by
Below threshold, generation is suppressed and the query escalates to a person. Escalation rate is monitored, not minimised.

The PRD goes further, with bad source data, chunking, retrieval misses and user misuse, each with its own detection and fix. Read the PRD.

Latency

Fresh answerabout 1.5 s
Semantic cache hitabout 200 ms

Staff repeat near identical questions daily. A match at or above 0.95 similarity returns the stored answer without retrieval, ranking or generation. At an assumed 40% hit rate, blended cost falls to $0.0014 per query. The hit rate is measured in closed beta.

The cost is not in running the model. The search index sits on servers that stay switched on, costing roughly two orders of magnitude more than the entire token bill. The lever is how the index is set up, not which model is picked, which is why the design keeps it to one pinned region and as few nodes as possible. Exact node pricing is confirmed at provisioning.

Running the spec

Prototype tested against the requirements

Built in Google AI Studio on Gemini 2.5 Flash at temperature 0.0, against a nine document knowledge base.

The most informative pass was a refusal. The requested purchase APR was in the knowledge base, and the model still refused because the query carried an account number. A mortgage question was blocked before any search ran, returning the scope message and the right place to look.

Defect 1

Word cap and citations

The happy path hit the 120 word cap once citation text counted, because my spec never said whether it did. Amended to "excluding citation text".

Defect 2

Citation format

The model added a document version to each citation. That beat my spec, so the spec now requires the version field.
