Problem Statement

How it started

Five years behind a branch desk, the same thing happened every week. A client asks what a card actually covers, and answering means leaving the conversation to search a dense PDF, then saying a number out loud from memory with nothing to back it up.

The product

Lumi Cards is an AI assistant for personal bankers and tellers, built into the portal they already use. It answers credit card policy questions using only approved documents, and every figure it states carries a link to the source and the date it takes effect.

The business problem is cost and exposure at once. Each lookup takes 4 to 6 minutes of live appointment time, leaves no audit trail, and risks misstating a regulated disclosure.

Who asks, and what it costs

Personal bankers handle the multi-clause questions where a wrong answer carries compliance exposure. Tellers handle higher-volume, single-fact lookups.

What did I find?

  • A customer-facing staff member sees 20 to 25 clients a week, 6 to 8 of whom raise a specific card policy question.
  • Across a branch team of 11 to 15, that is 60 to 90 lookups per week at 4 to 6 minutes each.
  • No audit trail. The answer is delivered verbally, from memory, with no citation confirming it before it is said.
  • Volume and duration are estimates drawn from five years across three Canadian banks, and are labelled as estimates rather than measurements.

Why retrieval was the only viable approach

Three other options were considered and rejected before this approach was chosen.

The vocabulary gap is the concrete one. Staff say "foreign conversion fee" and the document says "FX transaction mark-up", so a keyword search returns nothing useful at the moment it is needed.

The decision. Retrieval is the only approach satisfying both binding constraints: the corpus changes continuously, and every answer must trace back to an approved source document.

Solution & Process

The decision that does most of the work

Every piece of a document is tagged with the card name, document type, province, effective date, expiry date and source link. The system filters on those tags when a question is asked, rather than relying on instructions to the model.

That is the difference between asking a model not to quote an expired Quebec rate and putting that document out of its reach altogether. Wrong answers are blocked by design, not merely discouraged.

Three guards, in order

  • Before the search. A privacy check blocks customer data, and a scope check blocks questions the assistant is not meant to answer, both before any search runs.
  • During the search. The tags narrow the documents first, two search methods then run together, and a final ranking step passes on only the best five results.
  • After the answer is written. Every source the answer cites is checked against the documents actually supplied. If it cites something that was never there, the answer is blocked instead of shown.

What it refuses

Three kinds of question the assistant refuses, each stopped before the search runs rather than declined afterwards.

  • Out of scope. Non-card products are blocked by the intent classifier, which returns the scope statement and links to the right library. The refusal is a redirect, not a dead end.
  • Customer data. Account numbers and SINs are blocked before the question is processed, searched, answered or logged.
  • Low confidence. When the system is not sure enough about what it found, it gives no answer and sends the question to a person.

Escalation rate is monitored but deliberately not minimised. Reducing refusals means lowering the confidence threshold, which is the exact failure the controls exist to prevent.

Six ways it breaks

Each failure mode is paired with how it is detected and what fixes it, because an undetectable failure is not a managed one.

  • The worst case is a correct citation with a wrong number. The assistant finds the right insurance clause but states the limit wrongly. The source checks out and the link works, so staff read a wrong figure with full confidence.
  • The fix is a hard rule. Every figure, rate, percentage and duration in the answer must appear word for word in the documents it drew from. Anything that does not blocks the answer.
  • Split clauses are the subtle one. A coverage limit in one part of a document, with its exclusions pages away, produces an answer that is sourced, cited and still seriously misleading. Exclusions are tied to the clause they belong to, so pulling one pulls the rest.
  • Over-trust is tracked. Low citation click-through at high usage is the more dangerous reading, not the reassuring one.

Impact & Metrics

What a query actually costs

About $0.0024 CAD per query. A 13-person branch team runs under $1.10 a month in tokens.

The cost is not in running the model. The search index sits on servers that stay switched on around the clock, costing roughly a hundred times more than all the model usage put together. The real lever is how that index is set up, not which model is picked, which is why the design keeps it to one region and as few servers as possible.

Reusing past answers is where the saving comes from. Staff ask nearly the same questions every day, so a close enough match returns the stored answer and cuts the wait from about 1.5 seconds to about 200 milliseconds.

Running the spec

Built in Google AI Studio on Gemini 2.5 Flash at temperature 0.0, against a nine-document knowledge base.

  • All three refusal strings reproduced exactly.
  • The most informative pass was a refusal. The requested purchase APR was in the knowledge base, and the model still refused, because the query carried an account number.
  • A mortgage question was blocked before any search ran, returning the scope message and naming the right place to look instead.

Two spec defects, found by running the spec

  • The happy-path answer broke the 120-word cap once citation text was counted, which exposed that my own spec never said whether citations counted towards it. Amended to "excluding citation text".
  • The model added a document version to the citation format. That is better than what I specified, so the spec was updated to require it rather than leaving it to the model's discretion.

Recognition

Ranked 4th out of 77 PRDs in HelloPM Cohort 51, Assignment 3.

Scored 87/100 on a single rubric with no curve. The assessment credited the cost analysis approach and the prototype's faithful execution of the spec, while flagging gaps in cost sourcing and an unspecified confidence threshold.

Reflection

What the assessment flagged, and I agree with

The cost section is the one part of the document a reader cannot check.

  • The prices carry a date but no publisher.
  • The index cost, which I correctly identify as the larger figure, is never actually computed.
  • The confidence threshold is referenced in three places without ever being given a number.

Scope and disclaimer. A large Canadian bank is used as a worked example. Every source is publicly available credit card documentation. This is an independent study, not a project of any bank, and is not affiliated with or endorsed by one.
