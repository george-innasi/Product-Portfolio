Problem statement

Undergraduates in their first to third year who want to explore different causes struggle to find suitable, low-commitment volunteer options in one place, because existing aggregators and recruitment systems are built to fill specific NGO rosters or serve the general public, not to give students personalised feeds.

A volunteering platform that matches UG sophomores with Quests nearby their home or college based on their interests and availability.

  • Product & AI Architecture. Designing a rules-based matching baseline with Gemini 2.5 Flash re-ranking on top, backed by a Neon Postgres data layer, and deciding what to cut before the deadline.

I took Side Quest from first interview to a live pilot as the only builder, with no engineering team, in August and September 2026.

How it started

Charu's search raised a question we could not let go. If someone with a decade of volunteering history finds the route in this exhausting, how do first and second year students find it at all?

That question became the project. Not a lack of supply, but a failure of distribution: the opportunities exist, the intent exists, and the path between them is broken.

The product

Side Quest is a student micro-volunteering platform for undergraduates in their first to third year in Chennai. It matches them to local causes by interest, availability and travel distance.

The problem it addresses is distribution, not supply. Opportunities exist and students want them, but the route in is invisible, so intent decays before any platform returns an answer.

What the incumbents got wrong

A live audit of Indian volunteer platforms in September 2026, to see what the incumbents actually do.

What did I find?

  • Built for NGO rosters. Designed around NGO rosters and CSR reporting, not for a student choosing how to spend a free afternoon.
  • Excessive gating. One asked for job title and building number before showing a single listing.
  • Broken data filters. Another's Chennai filter returned results in Dadri and Mumbai. A third showed expired 2024 events inside a 2026 window.
  • No student-first discovery. None organise by student interest, schedule or availability.

Hybrid AI Matching: Rules-Based Speed with Gemini Reordering

Architecture

How Side Quest was built

  • Rules-based baseline. A score across interests, skills, availability and distance renders the quest board immediately.
  • Gemini 2.5 Flash reordering. The model then reorders the board and supplies a reason for each quest.
  • 1.5 second latency fallback. If the model is slow or unavailable, the baseline board stays useful and the fallback takes over once latency passes 1.5 seconds.

North star: confirmed quest starts. A student matched, attended, and logged their first civic hour. It sits past sign-up on purpose, because the usual failure is students who register and never turn up.

Feature

Quest Log

What it is. A shareable profile card that students can send to friends, family or prospective employers, consolidating everything that matters into one portable record.

What it shows. Causes the student is questing for, personal motivations, impact hours logged, quests completed, quest lines across organisations, and earned badges.

The insight behind it. Hours, milestones and verified badges should aggregate into one cross-organisational log rather than sitting as PDF certificates inside one NGO. A student who volunteers across three organisations should not need three separate proofs.

Pilot Learnings: What 6 Students Taught Us & HelloPM Recognition

Pilot cohort

What six students reported

6/6Would return within a month
6/6Rated account creation very easy
4/6Found quests a genuine fit
3/6Said only some quests fit their free times

Retention intent was unanimous. All six would return within a month, and all six found account creation very easy.

Schedule fit was the weaker signal. Interest matching landed, but only 37 quests was too thin for availability filtering to work.

User feedback

What it exposed

The redirect breaks the flow. A student who has just given Side Quest their interests, availability and location is sent to the organisation's own page to sign up again from scratch. This is the clearest defect in the product.

An unexplained limit. A user picked nine interests and hit a cap of five with no reason given. A constraint with no stated reason reads as a bug.

PM Reflection: What Six Users Taught Us About Real Product-Market Fit

Roadmap

Four priorities, in order

The roadmap is sequenced by what the pilot exposed. Direct apply and cohort feedback close defects six real users found. Live matching and AI reordering extend what already works.

  1. 1
    Live Profile Matching. The feed re-ranks as availability and interests change, not from a one-time onboarding snapshot.
  2. 2
    Supply Side and Direct Apply. NGOs post against declared student demand. Students apply inside Side Quest, with no redirect.
  3. 3
    Cohort Feedback. Close the onboarding friction and match-relevance gaps the pilot cohort raised, before adding campuses.
  4. 4
    AI as the Matching Engine. AI ingests unstructured NGO posts into listings, reads plain-language intent, and learns from every skip.

The rule. Fix the first campus before adding a second. No expansion until the first cohort reaches 50 active students and the redirect problem is gone.

Honest status. Side Quest is a working pilot, not a finished product. The listings and organisations are real, but they were gathered by hand at one point in time. Six pilot users is enough to find defects and nowhere near enough to claim product-market fit.
