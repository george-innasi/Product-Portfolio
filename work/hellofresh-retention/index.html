How it started

The brief was to pick a product, investigate it like a detective, learn from real users, find the one problem worth solving, and design the answer end to end.

I picked HelloFresh because the loudest complaint in every public forum was price, and price is almost never the root cause. That made it a good test of whether I would follow the evidence or the volume.

Overview

A retention study of HelloFresh, a meal-kit subscription. The audience is existing subscribers, not prospects. The business problem is that long-tenured subscribers, the ones who already survived the high-risk early months and have just become profitable to retain, churn without ever formally complaining.

Initial Research

What subscribers actually said

128 survey respondents, plus interviews and a Reddit thread of cancellation posts.

What did I find?

  • 48 per cent named a lower price as the single fix they wanted.
  • 18 per cent actively compared the box against grocery costs.
  • 15 per cent gamed the cancel and resubscribe cycle to get new-customer pricing.
  • 53 per cent had been charged for at least one delivery they did not intend to receive.

Price was the loudest complaint. It was not the root cause, and the rest of the study is about why.

Survey findings, 128 respondents

The Root Causes

Five causes under one complaint

Subscribers struggle to keep feeling the per-serving price is worth paying, week after week. Five things drive that, and only one is addressable by a product change.

  • Ingredient quality quietly declines and is unverifiable by the subscriber.
  • The price gap versus groceries widens with each increase.
  • Accumulated subscription value is invisible.
  • Long-tenured subscribers feel unrecognised next to discounted new customers.
  • The weekly skip decision repeats with no proactive intervention point.

Only the first can be fixed by a feedback mechanism, which is why Food Quality Feedback became the primary build rather than a discount.

Four confirmed problems, each scored out of 15 on population, intensity and frequency.

The two elevens were separated on evidence quality rather than the total. The accidental charge is sharp and trust-breaking but episodic. The AI content problem is high intensity in a vocal segment, and its population score was inference rather than measurement, which is stated rather than hidden.

What I deliberately parked

  • Delivery failures. More than five cancellations named delivery, across three countries. Parked because the cause sits in courier partnerships, not the product.
  • Loyalty pricing inequity. Parked because the fix sits in pricing strategy rather than the product.

The Solutions

Food Quality Feedback, and three supports

The primary build converts an unstructured support ticket into data the business can act on.

  • Food Quality Feedback. Subscribers report a specific ingredient issue, wilted, bruised or spoiled, traced to the supplier batch.
  • Accumulated value dashboard. Meals cooked, time saved, money saved, waste avoided, surfaced at the moment of the weekly skip decision.
  • Basil, a proactive assistant. In-app check-ins with subscribers who are disengaging, rather than waiting for the cancellation flow.
  • Milestone recognition at three, six, nine and twelve months, for the long-tenured subscribers sitting beside discounted new customers.
